# Reflections on AI from 2022 to 2026

The last four years in AI have been a whirlwind. From the early iterations of GPT-3 to the widespread enterprise adoption of fine-tuned Foundation Models, the industry has shifted from **"Prompt Engineering"** to proper **"Context Engineering"**.

I started my journey back in biology, but the draw to solving complex abstract logic puzzles using ML frameworks was too strong. I didn't want to just theorize—I wanted to build systems that actually ship and solve a real business need.

## The Shift in Engineering

When I was interning and building applications, I noticed a huge gap between *demo-ware* and *production-ware*. Everyone can wrap a layer around an LLM, but making it highly reliable, lowering the latency, and integrating it tightly into deep enterprise tools (like we did for the defect triage classifier) takes actual Software Engineering principles.

### Key Learnings:
- **Clean Systems over Hype:** It's tempting to jump on the newest agentic framework, but sometimes a well-placed RegEx and a simple classifier outperforms a sprawling LLM chain.
- **Architectural Tradeoffs:** Learning to weigh the inference cost versus the accuracy gain completely reframed my perspective on AI in production.

I'm incredibly curious to see where the next 4 years take us. Until then, I'm just going to keep building cool things.
